# OpenAI WebMCP Challenge brief

Verified against the [official OpenAI challenge page](https://openai.com/webmcp-challenge/) and [Devpost official rules](https://webmcp.devpost.com/rules) on August 27, 2026.

## Schedule

- Registration and submission period: August 25, 2026 at 11:00 a.m. PT through **September 3, 2026 at 1:00 p.m. PT**.
- Judging: September 4 at 10:00 a.m. PT through September 21 at 5:00 p.m. PT.
- Winners: on or around September 23 at 2:00 p.m. PT.
- Optional office hours: August 31 at 11:00 a.m. PT.

Treat the Devpost countdown and official rules as authoritative if any supporter page displays a different time.

## Eligibility summary

- Individuals must be the age of majority where they live.
- Individuals, teams, and organizations may enter; teams and organizations appoint one eligible representative.
- Participants must be in an OpenAI API-supported location and not in a territory excluded by the official rules.
- Teams have no size cap. One person may participate in multiple eligible entries.
- No purchase or specific paid OpenAI product is required.

Read the full rules before entering; this file is a working checklist, not legal advice.

## What we must build

- A web app powered by WebMCP where people and agents interact, collaborate, and create together.
- It must work consistently as shown and be accessible to judges in ChatGPT's in-app browser or Chrome 149+ with WebMCP testing enabled.
- New apps are encouraged. Pre-existing apps must have been meaningfully extended with WebMCP after the submission period began, with dated evidence separating new and prior work.
- Third-party SDKs, APIs, data, and assets must be used under valid licenses or authorization.

## Required submission materials

- A working live URL, free for judges to access through the end of judging. Authentication is allowed only if testing credentials are supplied.
- An English project description explaining:
  - why the use case is a strong fit for WebMCP;
  - how it improves the user experience;
  - what the person and agent can do together that was previously difficult or impossible;
  - how WebMCP was implemented.
- A **public** GitHub, GitLab, or Bitbucket repository containing all functional source, assets, and instructions.
- An open-source license that is detected and visible on the repository page.
- A public YouTube demonstration video under three minutes. It must show the working project and include audio explaining the project and its use of WebMCP. Do not use unlicensed music, trademarks, or copyrighted material.

Judges are not required to build or test the app; the live experience, README, description, images, and video must each stand on their own.

## Judging

There is first a pass/fail check for baseline viability, theme fit, and use of the required technology. Passing projects are scored on four equally weighted criteria:

1. **WebMCP leverage** — skillful, working, non-trivial use reflected in the code.
2. **Execution** — a complete, coherent product experience rather than only a proof of concept.
3. **Potential impact** — a credible, specific problem and audience that the demonstrated product actually serves.
4. **Creativity and ambition** — a novel concept meaningfully different from existing work.

WebMCP leverage is the first tie-breaker.

## Submission freeze

Before the deadline, drafts may be edited. The challenge FAQ says that once submissions close, do not change the Devpost submission, submitted repository, or live site until winners are announced; changes during judging can affect eligibility. If development must continue, fork the submitted repository and leave the judged version untouched.
